Unit 2 Understand data warehouses
Core Definition & Process
A data warehouse is a centralized, structured store optimized for analytical queries and
reporting rather than day-to-day transactions. Building one typically involves:
● Data Ingestion: Moving data from source systems.
● Data Storage: Storing data in an analytics-optimized format.
● Data Processing: Transforming data for analytical tools.
● Data Analysis & Delivery: Gaining and delivering business insights.
Dimensional Modeling (Tables)
Data is organized using dimensional modeling to support efficient analysis of large data
volumes through two main types of tables:
● Fact Tables: Contain the primary numerical data and metrics you want to analyze
(e.g., total sales revenue). They usually have a large number of rows.
● Dimension Tables: Provide descriptive context for the data in fact tables (e.g.,
customer details, store locations). They use two types of keys:

-- 68 of 118 --

○ Surrogate Key: A unique identifier internal to the data warehouse for
consistency.
○ Alternate Key: A natural or business key (like a product code) to maintain
traceability back to the source system.
Specialized Dimensions
● Time Dimensions: Enable analysts to aggregate data over specific intervals (years,
quarters, months, days).
● Slowly Changing Dimensions (SCD): Track changes to attributes over time (e.g., a
customer changing their address) to ensure accurate historical reporting.
Schema Designs
Unlike transactional databases that normalize data to reduce duplication, data warehouses
use denormalized data to minimize complex query joins.
● Star Schema: The most common design where a central fact table connects directly
to surrounding dimension tables. Microsoft provides specific design guidance in What
is a star schema? for Fabric environments.
● Snowflake Schema: An extension of the star schema where dimension tables are
further split (normalized) into secondary dimension tables to handle complex
hierarchies (e.g., breaking a product table down into categories and suppliers).
